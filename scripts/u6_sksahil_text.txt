• Monitoring & Controlling is not just a late phase; it can run in parallel with much of the project.

• Project scope = work needed to deliver the solution; product scope = features/functions of the solution.
• PMBOK process groups can overlap and interact; the guide does not force one rigid life cycle.

3. PRINCE2 - 7 PRINCIPLES, 7 THEMES, 7 PROCESSES
PRINCE2 stands for Projects in Controlled Environments. The course book presents it as a process-based methodology widely 
used in Europe and as a UK-government standard approach. Its core characteristics are continued business justification, a 
defined project organization, product-based planning, tailoring, and dividing work into manageable stages.
3.1 Four levels of PRINCE2 management
Level

Corporate / Program / Management
Senior organizational level

Project Board

without doing daily management.
Managing
Project Manager
Runs the project day to day and controls 
stages.
Delivering

Create/deliver the project solution and report 
progress.
3.2 Seven PRINCE2 principles
1. Continued business justification: A documented reason for the project must remain valid and be reviewed throughout the 
life cycle.
2. Focus on products: Outputs/products are clearly defined and agreed; product descriptions guide planning, change and 
acceptance.
3. Manage by stages: Break the project into controllable time periods and approve movement from one stage to the next.
4. Manage by exception: Each level delegates within tolerances and escalates only when boundaries are forecast to be 
breached.
5. Defined roles and responsibilities: Everyone should understand their role and the business, user and supplier perspectives.
6. Tailor to suit the project environment: Adapt PRINCE2 to project size, complexity, importance, capability and risk.
7. Learn from experience: Use and record lessons from past/current projects; the lessons log supports reuse of knowledge.
3.3 Seven PRINCE2 themes

PRINCE2 QUICK DIFFERENCE FROM PMBOK
• PMBOK is presented as more detailed and knowledge/process oriented; PRINCE2 is broader and higher-level with 
standardized roles/responsibilities.
• PRINCE2 gives particularly strong emphasis to business justification, products, stages, tolerances and management by 
exception.
• Both are process-based and suited especially to large projects, but they structure the body of knowledge differently.

5. AGILE SOFTWARE PROCESSES IN LARGE PROJECTS
The course book treats a large agile project primarily as one involving more than one independently operating team. Scaling 
agile can improve customer focus, speed, cost and responsiveness, but introduces longer planning horizons, 
authority/delegation issues, team synchronization, delivery integration and coordination challenges.
WHY SCALING AGILE IS HARD
• Large projects may have 12-18 month roadmaps while team backlogs are refined every 2-3 weeks.
• Higher-level change can arrive after teams have already completed detailed work, causing waste.
• Product-owner authority may conflict with program/portfolio responsibilities.
• Very large groups reduce the ability to self-organize and synchronize integration.
• Multiple teams create interfaces, dependencies and coordination overhead.
5.1 Scrum of Scrums (SoS)
• Divide the large project into multiple small agile/Scrum teams; the course book describes teams as fewer than 12 people.
• Each team selects an ambassador, often its Scrum Master, to join a cross-team Scrum of Scrums meeting.
• Meetings are short (about 15 minutes) and can occur daily or at a frequency the teams choose.
• Focus: team status, cross-team obstacles, interface/integration issues, responsibilities and work boundaries.
5.2 Large-Scale Scrum (LeSS)
LeSS attempts to scale the purpose and simplicity of Scrum. The course book characterizes it as an organizational design 
framework. Unlike SoS, LeSS views the large-scale setup as one Scrum rather than an additional management layer above 
multiple Scrums.
LeSS - KEEP THESE SHARED
• One product backlog.
• One product owner.
• One sprint.
• One definition of done for all teams.
• One increment at the end.
• Cross-functional teams.
• Sprint planning has Part 1 and Part 2.
LeSS nine core principles
1. Transparency: Tangible done items, short cycles, common definitions, working together and reducing fear.
2. More with less: More responsibility/customer focus/team ownership with fewer rules, artifacts and defined processes.
3. Whole-product focus: One backlog, product owner, shippable product and sprint regardless of team count.
4. Customer-centric: Learn real customer problems and use meaningful feedback loops.
5. Continuous improvement: Pursue ongoing improvement experiments.
6. Lean thinking: Eliminate waste, reduce partially done work, deliver quickly and delay decisions when useful to reduce uncertainty.
7. Systems thinking: Optimize the whole system and use system modeling rather than local optimization.
8. Empirical process control: Inspect and adapt product, process, behavior and organizational design instead of ritualistically following 
context-free practices.
9. Queuing theory: Use queue behavior to manage queue sizes, WIP, multitasking, work packages and variability.
5.3 Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe)
SAFe scales agile across portfolio, program and team/project levels. The course book emphasizes decentralizing decision-
making, economic thinking, systems thinking, variability/change and incremental learning while still coordinating at enterprise 
scale.
SAFe core competency (course book)
What to remember
Lean-agile leadership
Leaders model lean-agile values, coach/teach, empower teams and 
improve the system of work.

Lean strategy/investment funding, agile portfolio operations and lean

CALMR - DEVOPS MEMORY WORD
• C - Culture: shared responsibility for fast value delivery.
• A - Automation: reduce human intervention, improve speed and quality.
• L - Lean flow: limit WIP, reduce batch size, manage queues.
• M - Measurement: measure value flow to learn and improve.
• R - Recovery: enable fast rollback/fix-forward for production issues.
SAFe TERMS THAT CAN APPEAR IN MCQs
• ART (Agile Release Train): cross-functional agile teams operating on a shared cadence to deliver value.
• Solution train: integrates the work of ARTs for a complete large solution.
• Solution intent: repository for NFRs, interfaces, decompositions, requirements assignments, architectural guidelines and 
other alignment information.
• Multiple planning horizons: detailed near-term plans, less-detailed long-term plans - "plan to what you know."
• Business epics: large customer-facing initiatives; enabler epics: advance the architectural runway.
• At program level the course book describes 8-12 week iterative cycles and an innovation-planning sprint for innovation, 
inspection and adaptation.

6. SELECTING THE APPROPRIATE PROJECT MANAGEMENT METHOD
The course book rejects a one-size-fits-all methodology. Method selection should reflect the project and the organization. Off-
the-shelf methods may be convenient, but tailoring or combining approaches may fit the organization better if sufficient time and 
resources are available.
6.1 Organizational factors
Factor
Exam note
Functional structure
Specialization, clear functional control and economies of scale; but cross-
functional coordination/accountability can be weak and silos can cause 
friction.
Matrix structure
Dual relationships by function and project/product; enables cross-
functional coordination and scarce-skill sharing, but may create reporting 
friction and transient-team motivation issues.
Culture - power
Often small organizations; informal communication and norms of a central 
power source.
Culture - role
Often large bureaucratic organizations; coordination through clear roles/job 
descriptions.
Culture - task
Often matrix-type organizations; power lies at the intersection of roles.
Culture - person
Often professional organizations; the individual is the key element.
Mission / Vision
Mission = reason for existence; vision = where the organization wishes to 
be. Methodology should align with both.

Best fit / limitation

strong interdependencies exist, or

Accurate resource planning, but inflexible.

6.3 Iterative vs incremental vs agile
Model
How functionality grows
Iterative
All required features appear early at low but usable fidelity; later 
iterations increase fidelity until full.
Incremental
A few features are implemented at full fidelity first; later increments 
add more full-fidelity features.
Agile (as described in the book)
Starts with few features at low but functional fidelity; repeatedly 
refines and adds features until the complete solution reaches full 
fidelity.
TEXTBOOK EXAMPLES
• Space-shuttle IT / complex hardware-software system -> predictive is favored because midstream hardware requirement 
changes are costly and slow.
• Computer game with frequent user feedback -> adaptive is favored because the solution can be refined as feedback 
arrives.
• Most projects can combine methods; even predictive methods may have feedback loops, while a Scrum sprint can contain 
waterfall-like internal activities.

8. IMPORTANT SAQ - SHORT QUESTIONS WITH MODEL ANSWERS
Q. What is PMBOK?
Answer: A PMI guide that organizes recognized project-management knowledge, processes, tools and practices. In this course 
book it is structured around 47 processes, five process groups and ten knowledge areas.
Q. Name the five PMBOK process groups.
Answer: Initiating; Planning; Executing; Monitoring and Controlling; Closing.
Q. What is progressive elaboration?
Answer: The progressive detailing and refinement of the project management plan as more information becomes available and 
feedback loops clarify the work.
Q. What is project integration management?
Answer: The knowledge area that identifies, combines, unifies and coordinates project-management processes and activities, 
including resource trade-offs and interdependencies.
Q. What is the difference between project scope and product scope?
Answer: Project scope is the work required to deliver the solution; product scope is the set of features and functions that 
characterize the solution.
Q. What does PRINCE2 stand for?
Answer: Projects in Controlled Environments.
Q. What is manage by exception?
Answer: A PRINCE2 principle where each level delegates authority within defined tolerances and escalation occurs when those 
boundaries are expected to be breached.
Q. What is the purpose of the PRINCE2 business case theme?
Answer: To create and maintain the project business justification and keep expected benefits aligned with stakeholder 
expectations.
Q. What is a portfolio?
Answer: A strategic grouping of projects/programs managed together to align investment, resources and benefits with 
organizational objectives.
Q. What is a program?
Answer: A coordinated group of related projects, subprograms and sometimes related work managed together to achieve 
broader benefits and goals.
Q. What is a PMO?
Answer: A Project Management Office is a centralized unit that standardizes governance, shares 
resources/tools/methodologies, supports projects and monitors compliance.
Q. What are the three PMO types?
Answer: Supportive, Controlling and Directive.
Q. What is Scrum of Scrums?
Answer: A scaled-Scrum coordination approach where small Scrum teams send ambassadors to a cross-team meeting to 
resolve dependencies, interfaces and integration issues.
Q. How does LeSS differ from SoS?
Answer: LeSS treats large-scale development as one Scrum with shared backlog, product owner, sprint, definition of done and 
increment; SoS adds a coordination layer/meeting between separate Scrum teams.
Q. What does CALMR stand for?
Answer: Culture, Automation, Lean flow, Measurement, Recovery.
Q. What is an ART in SAFe?
Answer: An Agile Release Train is a coordinated group of cross-functional agile teams that operates on a shared cadence to 
deliver value through a continuous-delivery pipeline.
Q. What is solution intent?
Answer: A SAFe repository containing information such as NFRs, interfaces, decompositions, requirement allocations and 
architectural guidance needed to keep teams aligned.
Q. What is the difference between predictive and adaptive methods?
Answer: Predictive methods plan work and resources in advance under stable requirements; adaptive methods expect change 
and use flexible processes to respond to evolving or unclear requirements.
Q. What is an iterative model?
Answer: A model where all required features are available early at low usable fidelity and are refined across iterations until full 
fidelity.
Q. What is an incremental model?
Answer: A model where a small set of features is completed at full fidelity first, and later increments add more full-fidelity 
features.

9. MOST IMPORTANT 6-MARK QUESTIONS - MODEL ANSWERS
Q (6 marks). Explain the five PMBOK process groups.
Answer: Initiating authorizes the project/phase and establishes the charter and stakeholders. Planning defines scope/objectives 
and progressively elaborates the management plan. Executing performs the planned work and coordinates people/resources. 
Monitoring and Controlling tracks performance, measures variances and controls approved change. Closing formally completes 
the project/phase/contract, secures acceptance, captures lessons and releases resources.
Q (6 marks). Explain the three PMO types.
Answer: A Supportive PMO has low control and provides templates, best practices, training and lessons learned. A Controlling 
PMO adds moderate control by requiring compliance with frameworks, tools and governance. A Directive PMO has high control 
and directly manages projects. All can support shared resources, standards, audits and cross-project communication.
Q (6 marks). Explain PRINCE2 manage by stages and manage by exception.
Answer: Manage by stages divides a project into controllable time periods and requires review/approval before moving to the 
next stage. Manage by exception delegates authority from one management level to the next within defined tolerances. Normal 
work proceeds without escalation; a forecast breach of scope, cost, schedule or other tolerance is escalated upward. Together, 
they provide control without excessive senior-management intervention.
Q (6 marks). Differentiate portfolio, program and project management.
Answer: Portfolio management aligns investments and resources with strategy and judges aggregate benefits/performance. 
Program management coordinates related projects and work to achieve broader goals and benefits, providing overall leadership 
and managing cross-project change. Project management controls detailed execution and day-to-day work to deliver a specific 
result within constraints and satisfy stakeholders.
Q (6 marks). Explain the main challenges of scaling agile to large projects.
Answer: Large projects have longer planning horizons than team iterations, creating refinement mismatch and possible wasted 
work. Authority can conflict between product owners and program/portfolio leadership. Multiple teams create dependencies, 
interface/integration issues and synchronization needs. Large team sizes weaken self-organization. Distributed/virtual teams and 
compliance concerns add coordination overhead. Therefore tailored scaling frameworks such as SoS, LeSS or SAFe are used.
Q (6 marks). Compare Scrum of Scrums and LeSS.
Answer: SoS divides the project into small Scrum teams and uses ambassadors in cross-team meetings to coordinate status, 
interfaces, obstacles and integration. LeSS instead treats large-scale development as one Scrum and keeps one backlog, 
product owner, sprint, definition of done and increment across teams. SoS therefore adds a coordination mechanism; LeSS tries 
to scale Scrum with minimal additional structure.
Q (6 marks). Explain CALMR in SAFe.
Answer: Culture creates shared responsibility for value delivery. Automation reduces human intervention and improves 
speed/quality. Lean flow limits WIP, reduces batch size and manages queues. Measurement tracks value flow and supports 
learning/continuous improvement. Recovery enables fast correction of production problems through rollback or fix-forward 
capabilities.
Q (6 marks). Explain when predictive and adaptive methods are appropriate.
Answer: Predictive methods suit clear, stable requirements, strong interdependencies, regulated/safety-critical work and 
hardware-heavy systems where late change is expensive. They support detailed advance planning but are inflexible. Adaptive 
methods suit incomplete, unclear or rapidly changing requirements and cases needing frequent user feedback. They respond 
faster to change but provide less up-front certainty. A hybrid may be selected when both conditions exist.
